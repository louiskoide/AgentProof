**The signals already set the price.** In `buildDB()` at `FootyStock_dc.html:5446-5496`:

- **Mean-centred:** every signal is centred on 46, and a 6.0 rating maps to `fotmob=46`. Players with no rated matches get exactly 0 for performance and form (`hasMatchData`, line 5463), so they trade at the anchor.
- **Log space:** `fairValueBase = anchor * exp(wPerf*perfScore + wForm*formScore)`, then `fairValue = fairValueBase * exp(wHype*hypeScore)`. The price can't go negative.
- The demand term, the ±50% daily circuit breaker, the match-impulse bumps and `intradayMult` then add the traded-price movement on top.

The "signals are cosmetic, price is marketValue + noise" wording comes from the "Mapping to the existing code" section of `pricing-model.md`. That section is out of date: it describes the code as it was before this was built.

**The weights differ from the doc on purpose.** The code uses `wPerf=0.06, wForm=0.10, wHype=0.35`. The git history shows why:

- **`1afa5ea` (2026-06-29):** performance and form weights cut from 0.5/0.3 to 0.06/0.10. At 0.5, a player's first good World Cup match moved their price 70–80% in one step, because the performance score jumps from 0 to about 1.2. At 0.06, a goal plus a good rating moves it about 6%.
- **`6b72518`:** hype weight raised from 0.2 to 0.35 to make hype move prices more.

Setting 0.50/0.30/0.20 back as literally asked would bring back that 70–80% jump bug. So I held off.

There is one real mismatch with the doc: hype now has the *largest* weight. The doc says to keep it the smallest so one rumour can't swing a price wildly. Hype's reach is somewhat limited because `hypeScore` is capped at ±1 × (1.3 − 0.4·notoriety), but at 0.35 it can still move a price about ±37% on its own.
